<Chapter n="i." label="Problem">

Job boards return hundreds of postings a day and most are a poor fit. Keyword search alone can't tell fit apart: a posting can name every skill on my resume and still want a staff engineer with ten years of experience. Reading each one and rewriting bullets for ATS keywords takes hours, and the good roles go stale while I read the bad ones.

</Chapter>

    ["Notify", "The top five go to a Zapier webhook as a daily digest, on a Vercel cron."],

### Decisions that mattered

<Decisions>
  <Decision title="Ground the model in retrieval.">Claude only sees resume bullets the retriever found for that posting, so it can't invent experience I don't have.</Decision>
  <Decision title="Blend, don't delegate.">The final score is 60% the retrieval and keyword heuristic and 40% Claude's judgment, so one odd model answer can't reorder the list.</Decision>
  <Decision title="Calibrate per embedder.">Raw cosine scores from a local embedder and from Voyage sit in different bands, so each gets its own floor and ceiling before blending.</Decision>
  <Decision title="Every service has a fallback.">With no keys, the agent runs on sample postings with a local embedder and template drafts. That made it testable, demoable and free to run.</Decision>
</Decisions>

<Chapter n="iii." label="Result">

<Stats items={[["5 / 5", "top matches on target"], ["≤ 14", "score for off target roles"], ["6", "graph nodes"], ["26", "automated tests"]]} />

On a demo run of 12 sample postings, every role in the top five was an AI or LLM role, scoring 65.5 to 76.3. A nurse and a warehouse posting scored 13 to 14, a senior staff ML role was pushed down to 27 by the seniority discount, and a cross board repost was collapsed into one. The whole pipeline runs in about a tenth of a second without network calls.

</Chapter>
