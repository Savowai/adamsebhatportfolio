<Chapter n="i." label="Problem">

Football's rules are long, cross-referenced and amended every year. Fans, coaches and referees ask questions like "is that a handball?" or "is it a second yellow?" and get confident answers with no source.

A general chatbot makes this worse, because it answers fluently whether or not it has the text. The goal here is the opposite: **quote the current Law, cite the page, and refuse when the text doesn't cover it.**

</Chapter>

<Chapter n="ii." label="Corpus and ingestion">

<Stats items={[["348", "structured sections"], ["17", "Laws, plus VAR and glossary"], ["384", "embedding dimensions"]]} />

- **Structure-aware parsing.** The IFAB PDF is split by Law, section, subsection, definition, protocol and guidance, not by fixed-size windows. Each chunk keeps its full heading path, numbered lists, edition and PDF page range.
- **Pinned sources.** `sources.yaml` lists official hosts and SHA-256 hashes. A changed file fails closed until it is reviewed.
- **Safe re-ingestion.** Replacing a rulebook runs in one transaction under a per-book lock. An integration test forces a failure mid-insert and checks the old corpus is untouched.

</Chapter>

<Chapter n="iii." label="Retrieval">

PostgreSQL 17 with pgvector runs hybrid search: full-text plus local MiniLM embeddings, reranked by a cross-encoder. A query layer expands football slang ("handball", "second yellow", "DOGSO", "back-pass", "offside trap") into the rulebook's own vocabulary, and off-topic questions are refused.

A 12-question smoke test checked whether the right section landed in the top five: vector search found it for 10 of 12, hybrid for 9 of 12, at about 20 ms. That is a diagnostic, not a benchmark, and the repo says so. A held-out evaluation set is planned.

</Chapter>

<Chapter n="iv." label="Answers and deployment">

Locally, an Ollama model writes an answer that must cite the retrieved sections. Every citation is validated, and the pipeline fails closed if one doesn't check out.

That 4.7 GB model can't run in a Vercel function, so the public app uses deterministic corpus search instead: it retrieves and quotes the relevant rules and never generates a ruling. It needs no model key and has no per-query cost. FIFA, UEFA and Premier League regulations are the planned next sources.

</Chapter>
