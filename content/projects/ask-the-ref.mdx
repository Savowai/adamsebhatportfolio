<Chapter n="i." label="Problem">

Football's rules are spread across several documents that don't agree on everything: the IFAB Laws of the Game, FIFA's transfer regulations, UEFA competition rules and the Premier League handbook. Fans, coaches and referees ask questions like "can a goalkeeper score from a throw-in?" and get confident answers with no source.

A general chatbot makes this worse, because it answers fluently whether or not it has the text. The goal here is the opposite: **answer from the official text, cite the section, and say so when the text doesn't cover it.**

</Chapter>

<Chapter n="ii." label="Process so far">

The project runs in phases, and each phase has to be measured before the next one starts. Phase 2 is complete.

- **Sources as a manifest.** `sources.yaml` lists the five official PDFs with their editions. They are downloaded, never committed.
- **Section-level parsing.** The IFAB laws are split into their real sections, so a citation points to a numbered section of a Law, not a page number.
- **Hybrid retrieval.** Postgres 17 with pgvector, combining lexical and vector search, running locally in Docker.
- **Safe re-ingestion.** Replacing a rulebook happens in one transaction under a per-book lock. An integration test forces a failure mid-insert and checks that the old corpus is untouched.

### Measuring before answering

A 12-question smoke test checks whether the right section shows up in the top five results. Vector search found it for 10 of 12, hybrid for 9 of 12, at about 20 ms. That's a diagnostic, not a benchmark, and the project notes say so. A proper held-out evaluation comes before any generated answers.

</Chapter>

<Chapter n="iii." label="What's next">

Phase 3 adds generated answers that must quote the retrieved sections. Phase 4 is the full evaluation: multi-evidence recall, answer correctness and citation faithfulness. After that come the API, the web interface and deployment.

</Chapter>
